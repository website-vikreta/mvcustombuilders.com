# Target Users — MV Custom Builders

These are reasonable working assumptions for a historic/old-home renovation builder, not
confirmed research. Treat them as a starting lens, and update this file once the client
gives real input on who they actually serve.

## Primary: Owner of an Old or Historic Home
**Situational:**
- Owns (or is buying) a home with real age/character — pre-war, historic-district, or simply
  "old" — not a new-build.
- Facing a renovation, restoration, or repair decision on that home: a kitchen or bath that
  needs modern function without losing period character, structural issues (foundation,
  framing, roofing) that come with an old building, or a full restoration project.

**Pain they feel:**
- "A contractor who doesn't understand old construction will do more damage than good"
- "I don't want this house to lose what makes it special"
- "I've heard horror stories about renovations going over budget or over schedule"
- "I need someone who can work with original materials, not just rip everything out"

**What makes them hire us:**
- We understand old-building construction specifically — not treating it like a generic
  remodel
- We show real proof: before/after photography, close-up craftsmanship, specifics about
  materials and era
- The site and the work both read as sturdy, honest, and precise — not slick, not cheap
- They feel relieved to have found someone who won't cut corners or paper over problems

**Hero emotion target:** CONFIDENT. "This crew gets what this house needs."

## Secondary: Historic Property Manager / Institutional Owner
(Working assumption — confirm if this segment is actually a target.)
- Manages a historic building on behalf of an institution, trust, or commercial owner.
- Needs a contractor who can navigate preservation requirements, permitting, and
  documentation, not just execute the physical work.
- Judges by track record on comparable historic projects and ability to work within
  preservation constraints.

---

## What Both Share
- Trust is earned by evidence (real project photos, specific material/era detail), not by
  claims.
- Mobile-first behavior — research happens on a phone.
- Dense, vague copy reads as evasive on a trade site; short, specific copy reads as
  competent.
